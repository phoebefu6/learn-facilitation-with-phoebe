Close with decisions, owners and a record

A workshop can do everything right for eighty minutes and still change nothing, because the last ten were spent saying goodbye. The close is where a discussion becomes a decision people can point to: what was decided, by which rule, who does what by when, and when the group will look at it again. Tonight Larkfield's workshop closes, and you build the closing sequence and the record you will reuse in every meeting you run.

Where we start

It is 16:20 at Larkfield (constructed). The consent round in session 3 has produced a proposal with no remaining objections: four items, in order. Session 5's interruptions have been handled and noted. Jo has ten minutes. Kaner's guide gives closure a whole part of its own, from clear decision rules to reaching closure step by step, and the reason is practical: a decision nobody restated is remembered differently by each person who left the room.

Part 1 · covers reaching closure

Restate the decision, and the rule that made it

5 min live

Closure is not the last agenda item drifting into silence. It is a short, deliberate sequence: say the decision in words everyone can check, say which rule made it a decision, check that nobody holds an objection they did not voice, and only then write it down.

LiveRead the decision from the wall, and name the rule3 min▶

Jo reads the decision from what is written, not from memory and not in a paraphrase: "Items 2, 3, 5 and 8, in that order." Reading from the wall means the words being agreed are the words that will be recorded. Then Jo names the rule: "That is a decision by consent; the objections raised on item 5 were resolved by the amendment written during the consent round." Naming the rule tells everyone why this is a decision and not a sense of the room, and it ties back to the contract from session 1, where the rule was said aloud at 15:00.

Who reads matters. Session 4 modelled handing the close to the person who already spoke most: across the 40 real AMI meetings, the mean Gini of airtime rose from 0.198 to 0.236 on that model, and the summary arrived in the dominant voice. The facilitator reads the close from the wall because the facilitator has no stake in how it sounds.

Self-studyChecking, without asking "any objections?"3 min▶

"Any objections?" asked to a tired room at 16:21 produces silence, and Kaner's index carries a line worth remembering: silence is not an agreement. A check that works asks each person briefly, in a round, or uses a scale. Kaner's guide indexes a gradients of agreement scale (p.338) that lets people say how much they support a proposal, from wholehearted support through reservations to a block, instead of a yes or no; the exact wording of its points is in the book and not reproduced here.

The scale earns its place at the close because it reveals the person who will comply in the room and resist outside it. Someone at "I have serious reservations but will not block" is someone the owner of that item should talk to on Monday.

Real world

The decision everyone remembers differently. A familiar pattern: a meeting ends with "great, sounds like we are aligned", nobody writes the decision down, and a week later two people are executing two different versions of it, each sure the other was in the room when it was agreed. Nothing went wrong in the discussion. The close was missing.

Part 2 · covers owners and dates

One owner, one date, one first step

5 min live

Shared responsibility, Kaner's fourth value, has a practical shape: every action leaves the room with a named person who accepted it, a calendar date, and a first step small enough to start on Monday. A team is not an owner. "ASAP" is not a date.

LiveOwners accept in the room3 min▶

An owner is a person who said yes, out loud, before leaving. Assigning work to someone who is not present, or to a team, produces an action nobody feels. Jo asks for each item: "Who will own this?" and waits for a name, then asks that person: "What is the first step, and by when?" The date is a calendar date, not "next sprint", and the first step is small enough that the owner knows what they will do on Monday morning.

At Larkfield the owners come from both teams on purpose: item 2 to Ravi of Platform, item 5 to Mara of Growth. That split is a content decision the group makes; Jo only asks the question. It is also the practical form of shared responsibility: each team now owns part of the roadmap the other team wanted.

Self-studyWhere tracking begins, and this session ends2 min▶

Once actions leave the room with owners and dates, following them through a programme, status reporting and escalation is project-management work, taught in AI Project Management, Meetings to actions. The facilitator's job ends at a clean handover: the record goes to whoever tracks the work, and the facilitator does not quietly become the project manager.

Part 3 · covers the decision record

Write the record in the room

5 min live

The record is written while the group is still there, on a screen or a wall everyone can read, so that the people who made the decision also confirmed the record of it. Written afterwards from notes, it becomes one person's account of the meeting.

LiveWhat goes in, and what stays out3 min▶

In: the decision in the words read from the wall; the rule and who took part; every objection raised and how it was resolved; parked items with where they will be handled; each action with owner, date and first step; any reservations people chose to record; and the review date. Out: who said what in the debate. The record is of the decision and its reasoning, not a transcript, and a record that quotes the argument restarts it.

Objections and their resolution are the most valuable lines months later. When someone asks "why did we drop item 6?", the answer is in the record, in the group's own reasoning, and nobody has to reconstruct it from memory.

Self-studyWhy the review date matters most for consent2 min▶

Sociocracy 3.0's consent pattern lets a proposal go ahead when it is good enough for now and safe enough to try until the next review. That bargain only holds if the next review happens. Without a date in the record, "safe to try" quietly becomes permanent, and people who withheld an objection because the decision was reversible discover it was not. Writing the review date into the record, with an owner for calling the review, is what makes it reasonable to ask a group for consent rather than unanimity.

Send it the same day. Jo sends the record at 16:45 to the eleven people in the room and to Dana, the sponsor, with one line: "This is what we decided and how. If anything here does not match your memory of the room, reply today." A correction on day one costs a reply; a correction in week three costs a meeting.

Build-along

Larkfield's last ten minutes

★ 22 min · everyone builds

From 16:20 to 16:30 (constructed), Jo runs the close. Write the same five beats for your own meeting as you go, using the contract from session 1 and the agenda from session 2, and end with a record template you can reuse.

Restate from the wall. Read the decision as written. If the wording is unclear, fix the wording now, with the group, not afterwards.

★ Step 1 · 16:20 restate"Here is what is on the wall: items 2, 3, 5 and 8, in that order, as next quarter's shared roadmap. Item 1 stays committed as before and is not one of the four. Is that the wording we agreed?"

Name the rule, then check each person. A quick round, not "any objections?". Record anyone who states a reservation without blocking.

★ Step 2 · 16:22 rule and check"We said at 15:00 we would decide by consent. The objection on item 5 was resolved by the amendment in the consent round. Quick round, one word each: support, reservations, or object?" Record: names with reservations, and their reason in one line.

Owners and dates. For each item, ask for an owner, wait for a name, and ask the owner for a first step and a date. Write them up as they are said.

★ Step 3 · 16:24 ownersItem Owner First step By 2 Ravi ______________________ ____ 3 ______ ______________________ ____ 5 Mara ______________________ ____ 8 ______ ______________________ ____ Rule: a person, not a team; a calendar date, not "ASAP".

Park, and set the review. Anything raised and not decided goes on the parked list with where it will be handled. Then the review date, and who calls it.

★ Step 4 · 16:27 parked and reviewParked: next year's hiring plan (Ravi) -> Dana how Growth's targets were set (Mara) -> Dana (both written at 15:56, in their own words; session 5) Review: the whole group looks again on ________ (date); ________ (owner) books it today

Confirm the record and hand back. Scroll the record once while everyone watches, then close by handing the process back to the sponsor and the owners.

★ Step 5 · 16:29 confirm and close"This is the record, and it goes to all of you and to Dana at 16:45. If anything does not match your memory, reply today. From here the owners have it. Thank you: this was your decision."

Save your reusable record template. The seven sections from Part 3, blank, for every meeting you run from now on.

★ Step 6 · the templateDECISION RECORD · meeting: ________ · date: ________ Decision (as read from the wall): Rule, and who took part: Objections raised, and how each was resolved: Parked items, and where each will be handled: Actions (owner · first step · date): Reservations recorded (name · one line): Review date, and who calls it:

If 16:20 arrives without consent. The contract from session 1 already says what happens: Dana decides after hearing the two leads summarise the remaining objections. Jo still runs steps 1, 4 and 5, writing the objections word for word, so the sponsor decides on the group's reasoning. A fallback that was agreed at 15:00 is a rule, not a failure.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · It is 16:21 and Jo asks "any objections?". Nobody speaks. What should Jo conclude?

Kaner's index is explicit that silence is not an agreement. A round costs a minute and surfaces the person who would comply in the room and resist outside it. D is session 4's anti-lever.

2 · Which action line will most likely be done by its date?

A person who said yes, a first step small enough to start, and a calendar date. A team, a shared verb like "align" and an owner chosen outside the room all leave nobody holding the action.

3 · Why does a consent decision need a review date in the record?

People withhold objections partly because the decision will be looked at again. The review date, with someone who calls it, keeps that bargain.
